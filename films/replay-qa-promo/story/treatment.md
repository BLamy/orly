## Title

**Replay QA: { fully managed }**
Shelf subtitle: *End-to-end testing that explores, runs and records on its own*

## Logline

You give Replay QA one URL. It explores your app, turns what it finds into journeys and runs them on a schedule,
against staging, a preview or localhost, and on every pull request. It records every run. So when a bug only exists
*in time* (a 67 ms commit nobody clicked, one list fetched 258 times), it arrives with a recording and its root cause.

## The angle

The reference promo's spine, beat for beat: a haze with a half-finished thought, a bracketed promise, `{ introducing }`,
a prompt typed full-frame, a resolving step list, the product at work, a collage of proof with kinetic words, and a
tagline end card. Replay QA's **primary message** (fully managed, continuous, every run a recording) runs at full
volume through ch01–ch04 and ch06. The **hook** (bugs that only exist in time) lives in ch05, in the one dark register.
The **subtle beat** (developer and critic agents) is 7 unnarrated seconds in a corner at the end of ch05.

Time split (planned): primary message ≈ 58 s · time-travel hook ≈ 27 s · agent pair 7 s inside ch05.

## Voice of the narrator

Warm, confident, mid-life, unhurried but modern: a friend showing you something they're proud of, not an announcer.
Short lines, often fragments, each one landing on a cut. Numbers are said the way people say them; the exact symbol
(`67ms`, `−1`, `Tue at 3:00 AM`, `PR #3`) is on screen at the same moment, with a red-pink underline. The voice never
reads a list of acronyms or a URL. It drops out completely for whole beats (ch01-s02, ch03-s02, the agent pair, the
end-card hold) and lets the music and type carry them. It never imitates a real person. These ten lines set the tone:

1. "Imagine end-to-end tests you never had to write, or remember to run." *(an invitation, half-whispered; the sweep
   lands on "remember to run")*
2. "Point it at your app." *(five words, a beat of air, the kick)*
3. "No test suite needed. Just your URL, and a quick chat." *(easy, almost amused)*
4. "Then it keeps going. On a schedule, while you sleep."
5. "Against staging, a preview, even localhost." *(three quick taps, rising)*
6. "Some bugs only exist in time." *(slower, lower; the room goes plum)*
7. "Sixty-seven milliseconds. Four dropped frames." *(then silence: the music stops for four beats)*
8. "A screenshot can't see that. A Replay recording can." *(the film's pivot: a small smile on "can")*
9. "The bookmark never moved past minus one." *(deadpan)*
10. "Every run, a recording. Every bug, a root cause. Every fix, rerun to confirm." *(the line to remember, three
    even steps)*

## Recurring devices

**Narrative**
1. **The half-finished thought, finished twice.** "Imagine end-to-end tests you never had to write, or remember to
   run." The bracket `{ running on their own }` completes it in type; the end card completes it again,
   `{ End-to-end testing }` → `{ fully managed · always on }`. Promise, proof, payoff.
2. **"Every ___."** One grammar returns: *every run* a recording, *every recording* reviewed, *every pull request*,
   *every bug* a root cause, *every fix* rerun to confirm. It is the managed-platform message said as a rhythm.
3. **Time is the hook, not the subject.** Until ch05 the film moves at the pace of the calendar (3:00 AM, six
   Tuesdays). In ch05 time becomes the thing you can stretch, stop and rewind.
4. **Discovered, not scheduled.** Each bug page carries a small grey provenance label with its real discovery time
   and environment, so "some bugs only exist in time" is literal, and nobody assumes the 3:00 AM production schedule
   found either bug: `Discovered · Sep 23 · 5:56 PM · PR preview` (67 ms) and `Discovered · Sep 4 · 2:16 AM · Local`
   (258).

**Visual** (all from `look.md` and the promo kit)
1. **Haze progression:** `dawn` (ch01–ch02-s01) → paper `#e9e8ea` (ch02-s02–ch04) → `brand` plum (ch05 only) →
   `ember` (end card). Each register change is a `blur`; there is one `whip` (ch01-s03 → ch02-s01).
2. **Braces as the magnifier.** Kit `bracket` braces and `squareDots` frame type (`{ introducing }`), and in every
   product close-up they snap around the one detail that matters: the `Tue at 3:00 AM` chip, the `PR #3` badge,
   `67ms`, `258 times`, the `checkpoint −1` tag. The viewer learns that braces mean "look here".
3. **The red-pink underline.** As the narrator says an exact UI value, a hand-drawn red-pink underline runs under it:
   `Tue at 3:00 AM` (on "schedule"), `staging` (on "staging"), `PR #3` (on "pull request"), `67ms` (on
   "sixty-seven"), `258` (on "fifty-eight"), `−1` (on "minus one"). Braces snap on the downbeat; the underline lands
   on the word.
4. **The `▶ recording` chip: one object for the whole promise.** It pops out of each run row in ch04-s03 and the chips
   stack; the stack slides into the frame ribbon of ch05-s01; the developer hands one to the critic in ch05-s05; one
   rests beside the Replay mark on the end card. "Every run is a recording", seen rather than said.
5. **The caret.** Born on the URL typed full-frame (ch02-s01); it returns on the typed `qa.replay.io` on the end card,
   blinks twice and stops.
6. **Floating screens.** Every product shot is a `RrwebScreen` with a soft shadow and a 3-D tilt that settles flat as
   the narration names what it shows, cropped to the region that matters (≥ 900 stage px wide or zoom ≥ 1.8 for
   anything read).
7. **The frame ribbon and the rewind scrubber.** Only in ch05: a hairline ribbon of 16.7 ms frame slots (labelled as
   an illustration of the frame budget, never as product UI) and a bright playhead that runs *backwards*.
8. **Hairlines** drift across every haze frame, as in the reference.
9. **The ✓ clack.** Step-list checks land in green with a muted percussive clack (machines clack, never chime); the
   critic's "✓ good" echoes it, quieter.
