# Treatment A — "The Tick" (mystery, forward through failures)

**Logline.** A chat message gets its tick; the machine that gave the tick goes dark; the tick is still true.
The film follows that one promised message through every failure that could have broken it, and on the way
discovers that a plain storage bucket, the kind that cannot run a line of code, is the reason the room can move.

**Spine.** One illustrative message, #42 "the ferry is late", in one illustrative chat room, the harbour room.
Each chapter subjects the promise to a new failure (two machines claim the room at once; the owner pauses and
wakes; the owner crashes mid-upload; the successor dies mid-recovery; the tail lives only on neighbours) and
shows the single mechanism that saves it. History is not a preamble: each mechanism arrives with the people and
the date it came from (2020 one room in the world; 2021 the held reply; 2024 the log relay and the bucket
learning to refuse; 2026 celld and the commit).

**World.** A paper-cut harbour town at night. Machines are narrow lamp-lit houses on a hill. The chat room is a
paper lantern that lives in one house's window. The bucket is the town well, with a brass plate on its wellhead
naming the room's owner. The write-ahead log is a parchment ribbon that unspools from the lantern and is carried,
rolled into scrolls, down the well. Leases are candles on the windowsills.

**Device.** "Pull the pin": a red-tagged pin in each machine. Pull it and the scene replays without that
assumption, and we watch exactly what breaks. Then the pin goes back.

**Chapters (24 min).** 1 The Tick · 2 One Room in the Whole World (2020–2024) · 3 The Well That Learned to Say
No · 4 Two Lineages, One Commit (2026) · 5 The Plate and the Candle · 6 The Ribbon · 7 The Neighbours (celld)
· 8 What Travels.

**First 60 seconds.** Night; a long multiplane push across the harbour to one lit window. A woman's silhouette
types; a paper slip floats into the lantern; a small pause at the lantern's door; an ink tick lands. Silence.
The window gutters out. Up the hill, another window lights; a keeper lifts the same lantern out of the well's
bucket; the slip still carries its tick. Narrator: "The machine that made that promise is gone. The promise is
still true."

**Ending.** Dawn. The lantern in a different house; the slip with its tick; the well. "A tick is not a feeling.
It is a proof: the bytes were in the well, and the plate still named its owner when the bell rang."

**Five images.** The tick landing on the slip. Two keepers' plates colliding at the wellhead and the brass tooth
snapping shut on one. The cutaway of the well revealing a witness lamp deep inside. A keeper asleep at a window,
candle past its painted band, while another takes the plate. Two neighbours' notebooks under a wax seal, one
page missing, the room door staying shut.
