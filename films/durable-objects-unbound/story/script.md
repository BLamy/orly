# Durable Objects, Unbound
*One promised message, a well that learned to say no, and the road to running it yourself*

> A chat message gets its tick. The machine that gave the tick goes dark. The tick is still true. In a paper-cut harbour town where every machine is a lamp-lit house, a chat room is a lantern and the storage bucket is the town well, the film follows one promised message through every failure that could break it: two houses claiming the room at once, an owner that sleeps and wakes, a crash mid-upload, a successor that dies mid-recovery, a tail that lives only on the neighbours. From Durable Objects in 2020 to the October 2026 commit that began merging celld into workerd, it shows why a bucket that cannot run a line of code can make a room portable, and why it cannot do it alone.

## Chapter 1 — The Tick

*Cold open on one promised message. Across a made-up harbour, a chat room glows as a paper lantern in House A's window; a sender's slip, the ferry is late, pauses at the lantern's door and gets its tick. Then House A goes dark. Up the hill House C lights, draws the room up from the town well, and the slip still carries its tick. The machine that made the promise is gone; the promise is still true. Down in the square: a storage bucket, which cannot run a line of code, and a brass plate that names the owner. Two old promises, a newer third, and the question the film follows: how can a bucket make a room portable?*

## Chapter 2 — One Room in the Whole World

*2020 to 2024 on Cloudflare's network. Kenton Varda introduces Durable Objects: one identifier, one place in the world at a time, and a chat room as the demonstration. In 2021 his output gate holds every reply behind a latch until the write it follows has finished: the tick is a held letter, released. In 2022 the runtime, workerd, becomes open source, but its rooms cannot travel: a single instance. In 2024 SQLite moves into the same thread, local reads become almost instant, and Cloudflare's hosted Storage Relay Service keeps the tick honest with object storage and five followers, three to confirm. Real machinery, and all of it inside Cloudflare.*

## Chapter 3 — The Well That Learned to Say No

*The bucket's education, dated. Before December 2020 an overwrite in S3 could be followed by a read of the old copy; then reads became the latest, always. A race with the condition pulled out shows why fresh reads are not enough: an overwrite can win the well, but it cannot take back what was already served. In August 2024 the well learns to refuse a second creation; in November 2024 to refuse a stale replacement: compare and swap. Then the reversal: who referees the referee? Werner Vogels's witness inside S3, proved before it was trusted. celld's warning that a store can look compatible and silently ignore the condition, and the four test writes every node makes before it serves. And the scope: one key, one plate, no promise that two rooms commit as one.*
