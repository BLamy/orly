# Source notes

Reviewed 2026-11-06. These notes record the scope and important limits of the concepts illustrated; the examples are invented to show those mechanisms, not production measurements. Individual scene files cite their relevant pages.

## Workers and the Workers SDK

- [Fetch handler](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/handlers/fetch/) defines the module `fetch(request, env, ctx)` shape and the response contract; [How Workers works](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/reference/how-workers-works/) explains isolates. [Bindings](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/) and [Wrangler configuration](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/wrangler/configuration/) explain resource capabilities and configuration. [TypeScript guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/languages/typescript/) recommends `wrangler types` to generate configuration- and compatibility-matched types.

- [Best practices](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/best-practices/workers-best-practices/) warn against request-scoped state in globals and floating promises. [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) states `ctx.waitUntil()` can extend execution up to 30 seconds after response/disconnect; it is bounded and is not a durable delivery mechanism.

## Durable Objects

- [Concepts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/what-are-durable-objects/), [namespace](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/namespace/) and [lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/) describe addressable identities, stubs, placement and eviction. The first invocation, not stub construction alone, triggers instantiation.
- [SQLite storage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/) is recommended for new namespaces; it is private per object, strongly consistent and transactional. Point-in-time recovery covers up to the past 30 days. [Alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/) are at least once and retry on failure; effects should tolerate repetition.
- [WebSocket hibernation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/) keeps server-side client connections open while in-memory state is reset; the constructor runs again on wake. Socket attachments and storage enable reconstruction. Outgoing sockets do not use the hibernation interface.

## Data and storage

- [KV](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/concepts/how-kv-works/) is cached and eventually consistent; stale or negative cached reads are possible. [R2 consistency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/) is strong for direct object operations, while a separate custom-domain cache can return stale data. [R2 overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/) documents no Internet egress fees.
- [D1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/) is serverless SQL; [D1 read replication](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/read-replication/) documents session bookmarks and sequential consistency. [DO SQLite](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/sqlite-storage-api/) is private to one coordinator. [Hyperdrive pooling](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/concepts/connection-pooling/) manages connections to an existing database; [getting started](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/get-started/) identifies PostgreSQL and MySQL support.
- [Artifacts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/artifacts/) provides Git-compatible versioned repositories, requires Workers Paid and was announced as [open beta](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-10-01-artifacts-open-beta/). Its [Worker binding](https://developers.cloudflare.com/artifacts/api/workers-binding/) offers repository operations; standard Git clients work via its Git protocol.
- [Basin](https://developers.cloudflare.com/basin/) combines Pipelines, Catalog and SQL for analytics; the [SQL getting-started guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/basin-sql/get-started/) ingests records into an Apache Iceberg table on R2 and queries it. [Basin SQL](https://developers.cloudflare.com/basin/sql/) is generally available as documented; it is an analytic query service, not a per-request transactional database.

## Queues, K2 and Workflows

- [Queues delivery](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/reference/delivery-guarantees/) is at least once; idempotency is the application's responsibility. Batching, retries and dead-letter behavior are documented in [Queues configuration](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/).
- [K2 overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/k2/), [concepts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/k2/concepts/), [delivery guarantees](https://developers.cloudflare.com/k2/reference/delivery-guarantees/) and [limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/k2/platform/limits/) say that on 2026-11-06 K2 is public beta for Workers Paid, with 10 GB per account across streams, 20 streams, seven-day default retention, one-hour minimum and 30-day maximum retention by default; higher limits can be requested. A stream is ordered, but processing order is **not** guaranteed across workers sharing a subscription. Delivery is at least once per subscription, consumption does not delete records, replay is bounded by retention, and acknowledgments apply to batches. Consumers can miss records if they fall behind retention.
- [Workflows guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/get-started/guide/) and [sleeping and retrying](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/sleeping-and-retrying/) document persisted step results, retry policies, sleeping and resumption. A [Queue consumer can trigger a Workflow](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/trigger-workflows/), and a Worker can [produce records to K2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/k2/features/produce/). A retried step may repeat side effects; the sample composition is illustrative, with application-level idempotency required.

## Beyond a Worker isolate

- [Containers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/) and the [Durable Object Container API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/api/durable-object-container/) describe Linux-image execution and object-managed lifecycle. [Sandbox choices](https://developers.cloudflare.com/sandbox/concepts/) distinguish Linux Containers from JavaScript Dynamic Workers. [Sandbox security](https://developers.cloudflare.com/sandbox/concepts/security/) says all processes in one sandbox share its files/credentials, and the sandbox is the smallest trust boundary. [Dynamic Worker loader](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/getting-started/) documents `load()` and binding/network controls.
- [Browser Run](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/) and [Quick Actions](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/quick-actions/) distinguish hosted headless-browser tasks from controlled sessions. Worker `quickAction()` requires compatibility date 2026-03-24 or later and remote mode during local development; `/crawl` is REST-only.
- [Workers for Platforms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/get-started/) routes through `env.DISPATCHER.get(name)` in a dispatch namespace; [bindings](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/workers-for-platforms/configuration/bindings/) are attached to user Workers, and request-scoped capabilities can be passed through dispatch props.
